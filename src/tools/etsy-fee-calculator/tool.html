How Etsy works out each fee

Etsy charges most fees as a percentage of the order total: the item price times quantity, plus the shipping and gift wrap the buyer pays. The one exception is the payment processing fee, which is charged on everything the buyer pays, including sales tax or VAT that Etsy collects. These are the fees for a seller whose payment account is in the US; the calculator swaps in your country's numbers.

Currency conversion2.5%The sale, when your listing currency differs from your payment account currency

A worked example for a US seller: a $25 item with $5 shipping and no tax has an order total of $30. The transaction fee is 6.5% × $30 = $1.95. Processing is 3% × $30 + $0.25 = $1.15. Add the $0.20 listing fee and Etsy keeps $3.30, which is 11% of the order. If the same buyer arrived through an Offsite Ad at 15%, another $4.50 goes on top, so fees would be $7.80, or 26%.

Each line in the calculator shows its formula with your numbers, and each fee is rounded to the cent the way it appears on your Etsy statement. Fees are deducted from your payment account balance, so the money you are paid out is the order total minus these fees.

How to price for profit

  1. Add up the real cost of one order. The item (or the print provider's base price), the postage you pay, packaging, and what you spend on Etsy Ads for each sale. Ad spend is easy to forget: if you spend $30 a month on ads and they bring 10 sales, that's $3 a sale.
  2. Pick a target. A fixed profit per order (say $10) is good for low-priced items. A margin (profit as a share of the order total) is better when you compare products at different prices.
  3. Let the price finder do the maths. You can't just add your fees to your cost, because the fees grow when you raise the price. The finder searches for the lowest item price where the profit after every fee reaches your target, and shows the check.
  4. Check the break-even price. That's the price where you make exactly zero. Selling below it loses money on every order, even before your time is counted.

Three things that often surprise new sellers. First, fixed fees hurt cheap items: on a $5 sticker a US seller pays $0.20 + $0.25 in fixed fees, already 9% before any percentage. Bundles help. Second, free shipping doesn't save fees: Etsy charges the same percentage whether the money is in the item price or the shipping. Third, one Offsite Ads sale can wipe out the profit on a low-margin item, so price with a 12% or 15% sale in mind, or opt out while you can.

Print-on-demand on Etsy

With print-on-demand, your print provider (Printful, Printify, Gelato and others) charges you a base price for the product and a shipping charge for each order, and ships it straight to your buyer. Switch the calculator to print-on-demand mode and enter those two numbers from your provider's dashboard for the exact product, size and destination; they change by variant and by country, so check the one you're pricing. Packaging is left out because the provider packs the order. Remember that providers may also charge sales tax or VAT on what they bill you in some countries.
