How to make a journal interior

  1. Pick the trim size you will choose in KDP. It must match exactly, or KDP rejects the file.
  2. Leave bleed off unless something should print right to the edge. Plain notebooks almost never need it.
  3. Type the page count. More pages means a wider inside margin (the gutter), and the preview updates to show it.
  4. Choose a page style and adjust the line colour and weight. Flick through the spreads to check the left and right pages.
  5. Download the PDF and upload it as the manuscript in KDP. Then make the cover with the spine width shown above.

Why the lines stay inside the margins

Books are printed on large sheets that are folded, glued and then cut. Each cut can land a little off, so KDP asks for everything on an interior page to sit inside a margin: at least 0.25 in from the top, bottom and outside edge without bleed, and 0.375 in with bleed. Lines that reach into that zone can end up cut through on some copies and not on others, which looks like a printing fault to a reader.

The inside margin, next to the spine, is the gutter. Glue and the curve of the open book swallow part of the page there, and the thicker the book, the more it swallows. That's why KDP's gutter grows from 0.375 in for books up to 150 pages to 0.875 in for books over 700 pages. On a right-hand (odd) page the gutter is on the left; on a left-hand (even) page it's on the right. The preview shows both so you can check the mirroring. The KDP margin calculator has the full table.

If you do want lines to run off the edge, turn on bleed and tick Full-bleed lines. The page is then made 0.125 in larger on the outside edge and on the top and bottom, and the lines extend through that strip so there's no white sliver after trimming. Header text and page numbers still stay inside the safe area.

Lines that print well

A screen shows a 0.25 pt, 10% grey line perfectly. A print-on-demand press may not. Very light greys are printed as a fine dot pattern, and thin hairlines can break up or disappear. Lines at 25 to 35% grey and 0.5 to 0.75 pt look delicate on paper and print reliably on both white and cream stock. The tool warns you when lines are lighter than 15% grey or thinner than 0.5 pt. All lines are vector, so they stay sharp at any size.

Publishing a low-content book on KDP

KDP treats notebooks, journals, planners and log books as low-content books: pages meant to be written in. When you set up the paperback, tick the low-content option in the categories step. Low-content books don't need an ISBN; you can publish with none or with one you own, but they can't use KDP's free ISBN. Without an ISBN, KDP prints no barcode on the cover, and low-content titles aren't eligible for Expanded Distribution. KDP requires an even number of pages and at least 24, and larger trims have lower page limits (8.5 × 11 in tops out at 590 pages on white paper).

The spine width above uses KDP's paper thickness: 0.002252 in per page on white paper and 0.0025 in per page on cream. Paper colour doesn't change the interior file, only the cover. Spine text is only allowed on books with more than 79 pages.
