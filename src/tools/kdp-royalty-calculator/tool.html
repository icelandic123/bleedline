How KDP paperback and hardcover royalties work

For a printed book, Amazon keeps a share of the list price, then the printing cost comes out of your share:

royalty = royalty rate × list price − printing cost

The royalty rate is 60% when your list price is at or above the marketplace's threshold, and 50% below it. In the US the threshold is $9.99. KDP's own worked example, for a book that costs $4.60 to print:

List priceRateRoyalty
$9.9960%0.60 × 9.99 − 4.60 = $1.39
$9.9850%0.50 × 9.98 − 4.60 = $0.39

One cent off the price costs a whole dollar of royalty per copy. If your price is anywhere just under a threshold, raising it to the threshold usually earns more even if you sell a few fewer copies. The chart above shows the jump for your book.

How the printing cost is worked out

Printing cost is a fixed charge per book plus a charge per page. Short black-and-white books (up to 110 pages in the US) pay one flat price instead. The rates depend on the marketplace, the ink and paper, and whether the trim is regular or large: KDP counts a trim as large if it is wider than 6.12 in (155.5 mm) or taller than 9 in (228.6 mm). So 6 × 9 in is regular, but 6.14 × 9.21 in and 8.5 × 11 in are large and cost more per page. Amazon.com rates:

The minimum list price is the price where your royalty reaches zero: printing cost ÷ royalty rate. KDP won't let you list below it.

Expanded Distribution

Expanded Distribution offers your paperback to bookstores, libraries and other retailers. It pays 40% of the list price minus printing, so it needs a higher price to earn anything: a $4.60 book earns nothing there until the list price reaches $11.50. It isn't available for hardcovers or low-content books, and KDP pays it about 90 days after the month of sale.

How KDP eBook royalties work

eBooks have two royalty options:

  • 35%: 35% of the list price without VAT. Works at any price in the allowed range.
  • 70%: 70% of (list price − VAT − delivery cost). Only when your price is inside the 70% band, your eBook is at least 20% cheaper than its print edition on Amazon, and in the territories KDP lists for 70%. In Brazil, Japan, Mexico and India the 70% option also needs KDP Select. Books that are mainly public-domain content get 35% only.

Delivery cost is charged only on the 70% option: your file size in MB times a per-MB rate (US $0.15 per MB). A 2 MB novel pays $0.30 a copy; a 40 MB picture book pays $6.00, which is why heavy, low-priced eBooks can earn more on 35%. The calculator shows both options so you can compare.

Pricing tips

  • For print, check the price one step either side of the threshold. $9.99 often earns about $1 more per copy than $9.98.
  • Page count drives the printing cost. Trimming 40 pages from a black-and-white 6 × 9 in book saves $0.48 a copy in the US.
  • For eBooks, keep the price inside the 70% band and at least 20% below your paperback.
  • Compress images in heavy eBooks: every MB costs $0.15 per copy on the 70% option in the US.
