How to use the sudoku book maker

  1. Pick a difficulty and how many puzzles you want. With Pro you can build sections instead, for a book that gets harder as it goes.
  2. Press Generate. Puzzles are made in the background, several at a time on computers with more than one core, so the page stays responsive. 500 puzzles usually take well under a minute.
  3. Check the preview. Flip through the puzzles, tick Show solution to see the answer, and read which solving techniques each puzzle needs.
  4. Choose the layout and download. One puzzle per page is large print. Two per page suits most books. Four or six per page makes a compact pocket book or worksheet.

How the difficulty is graded

Every puzzle has exactly one solution. The generator checks this with a solver that counts solutions and stops at two, so a puzzle with two answers is never kept.

Difficulty isn't just the number of clues, because a puzzle with few clues can still be easy. Each puzzle is solved step by step by a grader that works like a person: it always uses the simplest technique that makes progress. The puzzle's level is set by the hardest technique it needs, and the number of givens has to fall in that level's range.

LevelHardest technique neededGivens
EasyNaked and hidden singles only36–40
MediumAt least one pointing pair, box/line reduction, naked pair or hidden pair28–35
HardAt least one naked or hidden triple, X-Wing or XY-Wing24–31
ExpertAt least one Swordfish, Jellyfish, XYZ-Wing, quad, simple colouring or XY-Chain22–28

Every puzzle can be solved by logic with the techniques above. None needs guessing. Puzzle rating is not an exact science, so a hard puzzle can feel easy to someone who spots the key step early, but the levels go up steadily and readers get what the label says.

Making a sudoku book for Amazon KDP

Low-content or not? KDP's help page lists puzzle books among the examples that are generally not low-content. That means you publish a sudoku book as a regular paperback: it can have a free KDP ISBN and you don't tick the low-content box. KDP's rules can change, so read its low-content page before you publish.

Trim size. 8.5 × 11 in works for large print and two puzzles a page. 6 × 9 in is cheaper to print and handy for travel books, with one or two puzzles a page. Four or six puzzles per page on 6 × 9 makes small grids, so check the printed cell size the tool shows.

Margins. KDP's minimum inside margin depends on the page count: 0.375 in up to 150 pages, 0.5 in up to 300 pages, 0.625 in up to 500 pages and more after that. The tool sets the gutter from the final page count and adds a little extra on every side, so nothing gets lost in the binding.

Page count. KDP paperbacks need at least 24 pages and an even page count. The tool adds blank pages at the end only when it has to. More puzzles are a better way to reach 24 pages than blank paper.

Why the solutions matter. Every sudoku book needs an answer key. Readers use it to check their work and to get unstuck, and a book without one draws complaints. The solutions section prints the givens in bold so it's easy to match an answer to its puzzle.

What KDP checks. On upload, KDP's previewer checks that the page size matches the trim and bleed you chose, that fonts are embedded, and that nothing sits outside the margins. The PDFs here embed their fonts and keep every page inside the live area. Make your cover separately once you know the final page count, because the spine width depends on it.
