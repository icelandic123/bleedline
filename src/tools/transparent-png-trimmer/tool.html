    Why transparent edges matter on a printed shirt

    On screen, a pixel at 3% opacity is invisible. A garment printer sees it differently. Direct-to-garment (DTG) printers put down a layer of white ink under the colors on dark fabric, and they decide where to print from the alpha channel: anything that isn't fully transparent can get ink. That's how a design that looked perfect on your screen comes back with a faint box around it, a scatter of grey dots, or a pale outline.

    Three things cause most of it:

    • Stray specks. Background removal, magic-wand selections and soft erasers leave tiny islands of pixels at very low opacity, often far from the artwork. They also stretch the design's bounding box, so services that scale by the box print your design smaller than you meant.
    • Fringes and halos. When artwork is cut from a white background, the anti-aliased edge pixels are a blend of the art and that white. On a white shirt nobody notices; on black or navy they form a thin light outline. A design cut from a dark background gets the opposite, a dark rim that shows on light shirts.
    • Large soft areas. Drop shadows, glows and faded textures are semi-transparent by design. Printers reproduce partial opacity with halftone dots or a thinner layer of ink, which can look grainy, and the white underbase can show through as a pale haze on dark fabric. Keep them deliberate and test-print if they matter.

    What each clean-up does

    • Remove stray specks deletes small separate bits that are either nearly invisible or well away from the artwork. Larger pieces and anything close to the design are kept, so the dot of an "i", a full stop or a small star near the art survive. Raise the size if you have bigger debris, and check the red circles first.
    • Remove near-invisible pixels makes every pixel below the opacity threshold fully transparent, including the faintest part of soft edges. At the default of 10 out of 255 (about 4%) nothing visible changes.
    • Clean up edge fringe keeps the edge's softness but replaces its color with the color of the solid artwork right next to it, so the white (or black) left from the old background disappears.
    • Harden edges turns every pixel either solid or fully transparent at the threshold you choose. Use it when a printer or a cutting machine (for stickers and vinyl) needs crisp edges.
    • Trim to the artwork crops the empty transparent border, with optional even padding.

    Is it really transparent?

    A surprising number of "transparent PNG" downloads aren't: the grey-and-white checkerboard is painted into the picture. The report's first line tells you whether the file has an alpha channel and how much of it is actually transparent. If it says no pixels are transparent, the background will print as a solid rectangle.
