How to use the word search maker

  1. Add your words. Type a title and a word list, or pick a ready-made theme. To build a whole book quickly, add all the themes, or paste many lists at once with a blank line between them.
  2. Set the grid. 10 × 10 to 13 × 13 with across and down words suits early readers. 15 × 15 with diagonals is a classic adult puzzle. 20 × 20 and up, with backwards words and filler drawn only from the words' own letters, is hard.
  3. Check the preview. Flip through the puzzles with the arrows and tick Show solution to see where every word went. If a word doesn't fit, the tool says so and suggests the smallest grid that holds them all.
  4. Download the PDF. Choose printable pages for a classroom or home printer, or a KDP book interior to publish as a paperback.

The seed makes results repeatable. The same words, settings and seed always give the same grid, so you can rebuild a book later and get identical puzzles. Change the seed (or press ↻) for a fresh layout.

What the generator does for you

  • Every word is placed, or you're told which ones aren't. It places the longest words first and backs up to try other spots when a word won't fit, so a tight grid still works.
  • No accidental doubles. Random filler can spell a list word a second time, which leaves solvers confused about which one counts. The tool checks every word in all eight directions and replaces filler letters until each word appears exactly once. The one exception is a word hidden inside another word (CAT inside CATERPILLAR), which the tool points out so you can change it.
  • No rude surprises. Filler letters are re-rolled if they happen to spell a short list of offensive words, which matters for classroom printouts.

Making a word search book for Amazon KDP

Is it a low-content book? Probably not. KDP's own help page lists notebooks, journals, planners and log books as low-content, and names puzzle books among the examples that are generally not low-content. In practice that means you publish a puzzle book as a normal paperback: you can use a free KDP ISBN, Expanded Distribution is available, and you don't tick the low-content box. Check KDP's current low-content page before you publish, because the rules can change.

Choosing a trim size. 8.5 × 11 in gives the biggest grids and suits large-print and children's books. 6 × 9 in is cheaper to print and easy to carry. Below about 9 pt the grid letters get hard to read for many adults, so the tool shows the letter size it will print at. Keep large-print books to grids of 15 × 15 or smaller on 6 × 9.

Margins and bleed. KDP needs a wider inside margin as the page count grows (0.375 in up to 150 pages, 0.5 in up to 300 pages). The tool uses the page count of the finished PDF to pick the right gutter and adds a little extra on every side. Puzzle pages don't print to the edge, so leave bleed off unless you plan to add edge-to-edge artwork. If you do switch it on, set bleed to "yes" when you upload too, since KDP checks that the page size matches.

Page count. A KDP paperback needs at least 24 pages and an even page count, so the tool adds blank pages at the end when needed. A book padded with lots of empty pages is a poor read, so add more puzzles rather than relying on padding.

Why the answer key matters. Solvers get stuck, and a puzzle book without solutions draws complaints. The answer key circles each word with a rounded outline and greys out the filler letters, so the answers are easy to see even when grids are printed small.

What KDP checks. When you upload the interior, KDP's previewer checks the page size against the trim and bleed you chose, that all fonts are embedded, and that nothing sits outside the margins. This tool embeds its fonts and keeps every page inside the live area. You'll make the cover separately: its width depends on your final page count and paper type.
