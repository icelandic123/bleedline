    How to use it

    1. Drop your design. A transparent PNG is best for anything printed on a product.
    2. Tick a preset, or choose Custom size and type the pixels you need.
    3. Check the preview. For shirts, Fit inside with Top keeps the whole design and puts it at chest height. For posters and listing photos, Fill covers the frame; drag the image to choose the crop.
    4. Download. The file has exactly the preset's pixel size, keeps transparency (PNG), and is labelled 300 DPI.

    Why every marketplace wants a different file

    Each print-on-demand service prints on its own blanks with its own print areas, so each asks for a different canvas. Sending the same file everywhere usually works, but the design ends up a different size or position on each product. Exporting to each service's dimensions puts you in control of where it lands. The presets use only numbers we found on each service's own help pages (linked at the bottom of this page); where a service publishes only a minimum, the preset uses that minimum, and bigger is fine.

    • Redbubble checks pixels only; the DPI label doesn't matter. Its minimums are, for example, 3873 × 4814 px for the men's graphic T-shirt, 2875 × 3900 for the premium T-shirt and 2400 × 3200 for other apparel. Files over 13,500 px on a side or 300 MB are rejected, and transparent PNG is needed for apparel and stickers. Stickers are best uploaded at 2800 × 2800 and art prints at 3840 × 3840.
    • TeePublic asks for a transparent PNG at 150 DPI, at least 1500 × 1995 px (not counting empty transparent edges), and at least 5000 × 5500 px to enable every product.
    • Spring uses 3720 × 4950 px for standard apparel, PNG, up to 50 MB.
    • Printful works from print areas in inches: 12 × 16 in for the standard DTG front, larger 15 × 18 in on some products. At 300 DPI those are 3600 × 4800 and 4500 × 5400 px. Printful asks for 150 to 300 DPI depending on the product, and no more than 300.
    • Spreadshop recommends at least 4000 px on the longest side, at least 1000 px on the shorter side, and files up to 10 MB.
    • Etsy recommends listing photos of at least 2000 × 2000 px (the first photo needs at least 635 × 635), in sRGB, and JPEG or PNG without transparency. Files over 1 MB can fail to upload on slow connections, so JPEG at quality 85 to 90 is a good choice.

    Pad or crop, and where the design sits

    Fit inside scales the design until it touches the frame on one side and fills the rest with transparency (or a color). Nothing is cut off. That's what you want for garments, stickers, mugs and phone cases, where the empty part simply doesn't print. Turn on Trim empty transparent edges so a wide border of nothing in your source file doesn't shrink the artwork.

    Fill scales the design until it covers the frame and crops what sticks out. Use it for posters, art prints, canvases, cards and listing photos, which print or show edge to edge. Drag the preview to choose the crop.

    The tool never stretches. Changing the shape of artwork to fit a frame distorts it, and printers will happily print the distortion.

    Upscaling, and why the tool warns you

    Resizing down throws away pixels you don't need, and done well it looks as sharp as the original. Resizing up has to invent pixels between the ones that exist. A good filter makes that smooth rather than blocky, but it can't create detail, so a design scaled up 2× looks soft when printed big. The tool shows the scale factor and warns above 1.5×. If you see the warning, export the design bigger from your design app (vector artwork can be exported at any size) rather than upscaling here.

    How the resizing works

    Browsers' built-in image scaling cuts corners when shrinking by a lot, which leaves jagged or shimmering edges. This tool resamples every pixel itself, in strips so even a 100-megapixel poster won't freeze the tab, using a bicubic (Catmull-Rom) filter widened when shrinking so that every source pixel counts. It works in premultiplied alpha, which is what keeps transparent edges free of dark fringes, and the filter never adds faint pixels outside your artwork's edges. PNGs are then written directly, so the output isn't limited by the browser's canvas size.
