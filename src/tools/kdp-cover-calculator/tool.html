How to use the cover calculator

  1. Pick your trim size, the exact page count of your finished interior PDF, and the ink and paper you'll choose in KDP. The spine depends on all three, so finish the interior first.
  2. Read off the full cover size. That is the page size of the PDF you upload: back cover, spine and front cover side by side, plus 0.125 in of bleed on every edge.
  3. Download the template. Put the PNG or PDF on the bottom layer of your design (or the guides-only file on the top layer), build your cover around it, then hide the template before you export.
  4. Export one PDF at exactly the full cover size, with images at 300 DPI or more. Fonts must be embedded.

How KDP spine width is calculated

A paperback's spine is as thick as its stack of paper, so KDP multiplies the page count by a fixed thickness per page for each paper:

Ink and paperInches per pagemm per page
Black & white, white paper0.0022520.0572
Black & white, cream paper0.00250.0635
Black & white, groundwood paper0.002350.0596
Standard color0.0022520.0572
Premium color0.0023470.0596

The full cover then follows from the trim size:

spine = pages × per-page thickness
width = bleed + back + spine + front + bleed = 2 × trim width + spine + 0.25 in
height = bleed + trim height + bleed = trim height + 0.25 in

Worked example: a 6 × 9 in paperback with 200 pages on white paper. Spine = 200 × 0.002252 = 0.4504 in (11.44 mm). Width = 2 × 6 + 0.4504 + 0.25 = 12.7004 in. Height = 9 + 0.25 = 9.25 in. At 300 DPI that is 3810 × 2775 pixels. The same book on cream paper has a 0.5 in spine, so switching paper changes your cover file.

Cream paper is thicker than white, so the same book gets a wider spine. That's also why you can't reuse a cover after adding pages: every extra sheet makes the spine wider, the front cover moves right, and the file no longer matches.

Why the cover is one PDF with bleed

KDP prints the back, spine and front on one sheet, wraps it around the book block and cuts it to size. The cutting blade and the fold both move a little from book to book, so:

  • Bleed (0.125 in, 3.2 mm, on every side): background colour and images must run past the trim line to the edge of the file. If they stop at the trim, a slightly-off cut leaves a thin white edge.
  • Safe area (0.125 in inside the trim): keep titles, author names and logos inside it so the cut never clips them.
  • Spine folds: the fold can land up to 0.0625 in (1.6 mm) either side of the line. Keep spine text 0.0625 in clear of each fold, and avoid a hard colour change exactly on the fold, or it may wrap onto the front cover.
  • Spine text: only allowed on books with more than 79 pages. Thinner spines are too narrow to print text reliably (KDP's Cover Creator asks for 80).
  • Barcode area: KDP prints a free barcode in a 2 × 1.2 in white box at the lower right of the back cover, 0.25 in from the spine and the bottom trim. Leave that space free of text and important art.

Because it is one sheet, the cover is one PDF page. Uploading the front cover alone, or a file sized to the trim without bleed, is the most common reason a cover is rejected.

Cover file checklist

  • Page size exactly matches the full cover size above (the template PDF is already that size).
  • Images at 300 DPI or more at their printed size. KDP recommends staying at or below 600 DPI.
  • Text at least 7 pt and 0.125 in inside the trim. Fonts embedded.
  • Template layer hidden or deleted before export.
  • File under 650 MB.
