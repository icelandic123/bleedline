Page sizes and margins

How KDP margins work

Every interior page has a safe live area: the part of the page where text, page numbers and anything else that matters must sit. Around it are four margins. The top, bottom and outside margins protect your text from the trimming blade, because the cut can land slightly differently on each copy. The inside margin, next to the spine, is the gutter.

Why the gutter grows with page count

Paperbacks are glued along the spine, so they never open completely flat. Near the spine the pages curve away into the binding, and the thicker the book, the deeper that curve. A 100-page book loses only a sliver of each page; an 800-page book swallows almost an inch. KDP sets the minimum gutter by page count:

These are minimums. Text set right at the limit looks cramped, which is why most trade books use 0.5 to 0.75 in on the outside and more in the gutter. Use the extra margin box to try a roomier page. If you add pages later and cross one of the thresholds, re-check the gutter: 300 pages needs 0.5 in, 302 pages needs 0.625 in.

Left and right pages are mirrored

Page 1 of a book is always a right-hand page, so every odd page is on the right with the gutter on its left edge, and every even page is on the left with the gutter on its right edge. That's why word processors call this setting mirror margins (Word) or mirrored pages (LibreOffice): the inside and outside margins swap from one page to the next.

What bleed is

Bleed is extra artwork beyond the trim line, there so the printer can cut through it without leaving a white sliver. On KDP, an interior with bleed is 0.125 in (3.2 mm) larger on the outside edge and on the top and bottom, so a 6 × 9 in book becomes a 6.125 × 9.25 in PDF (441 × 666 pt). There is no bleed on the gutter side, because that edge is glued into the spine. If any page bleeds, set up the whole file with bleed and choose "Bleed (PDF only)" when you upload.

Setting up Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice

The setup boxes above list the exact values. Set the page size before the margins, and apply both to the whole document. Word and LibreOffice have true mirrored margins. Google Docs doesn't, so the Docs values put the larger inside margin on both sides: you lose a little space on the outside edge, but the file is safe on every page. After writing, export a PDF and check that its page size matches the size here, with every font embedded.
